# Problems in the Personal Agent Protocol specification

Problems we found in [Personal Agent Protocol](https://personalagentprotocol.org/) (Poppy) Draft 0.1 ("Updated Oct 12, 2026"; the 12 October update changed no wire rule of the 9 October text these issues were found in), its Guides and the Operations extension v1, written so they can go to the spec's authors. The authors take feedback by email at feedback-personal-agent-protocol@sierra.ai, as inline comments exported from the site, or written with their agent kit ([Path to 1.0](https://personalagentprotocol.org/docs/path-to-1#feedback), 12 October). None has been sent yet; sending one needs the owner's approval ([SECURITY.md](../SECURITY.md)). Each has a suggested fix and says what our client does meanwhile. [readings.md](readings.md) records, for the S-numbered questions, which reading our client and our strict and lenient mock Companies take, and why.

## How we found them

- **Building a client.** We built a Personal Agent client and a strict mock Company that records a named violation for each check it makes (`test/mock-company/violations.ts`).
- **Running it under different readings.** Our conformance tests run under six readings of the questions below.
- **Reading other implementations.** We read the other implementations listed in [implementations.md](implementations.md); their issues are in [poppy-issues.md](poppy-issues.md). Their disagreements are often the evidence for an issue here.
- **A discovery-document validator** ([`src/validator/`](../src/validator)).
- **An interop grid.** Our [harness](interop-spec.md) runs agents against Companies from several projects, in containers, through a recording proxy; [a summary of the latest run](interop-results/README.md), from 13 October, is committed. Where two implementations that each pass alone fail together, the cause is usually one of the issues here, and the row says "Grid:".
- **A full crawl of the site on 11 October 2026.**
  - The sitemap lists nine pages: home, overview, spec, extensions, Operations, guides, open topics, license, contributions.
  - There's no `llms.txt`, changelog or public source repository; the only copy of the source is in poppyseed.
  - The home page's walkthrough traces exist only in a JavaScript bundle.
  - The spec has 107 sentences with RFC 2119 keywords, Operations has 20, and the Guides 138 bullet points, nearly all recommendations. Many more requirements carry no keyword, such as 4.4's "the Personal Agent starts a new Session" on `account_mismatch`. Which party each keyword sentence binds is a judgement; [spec/grounding.md](../spec/grounding.md#counts) lists them.
- **A snapshot of the site on 12 October 2026**, in [spec/draft-0.1-2026-10-09](../spec/draft-0.1-2026-10-09/README.md). Nothing had changed since 9 and 10 October, so nothing had changed since our crawl either. [spec/grounding.md](../spec/grounding.md) quotes the text each issue below rests on and gives a verdict; issues the text partly addresses, or that we had misread, say so in their rows, dated 12 October. Checked again on the evening of 12 October: no page's body or text had changed, and the site still links to no source repository or issue tracker.
- **The 12 October update**, stored on 13 October in [spec/draft-0.1-2026-10-12](../spec/draft-0.1-2026-10-12/README.md). It changed no wire rule: the spec's only change is a link in section 1, and Extensions, Operations and the Guides changed only their date. Overview became Introduction, and Open topics moved into a new page, [Path to 1.0](https://personalagentprotocol.org/docs/path-to-1), which adds the feedback process, the agent kit and the list of design partners. Every quotation in [spec/grounding.md](../spec/grounding.md#the-12-october-update) is in the new text, so every verdict stands. There is still no source repository or issue tracker; feedback goes by email.

**IDs.** S-numbers came from comparing implementations, N-numbers from the crawl and later work, including the grid. Our tests and docs cite them, so they don't change. Six were our own misreadings of the draft and are withdrawn: S6, S15, S21, N36, N37 and N38. Their numbers aren't reused, and [spec/grounding.md](../spec/grounding.md) quotes the text that answers each.

**Kind:**
- **Interop:** two conforming implementations can fail to work together.
- **Security:** a security or privacy risk.
- **Clarity:** underspecified, though implementations mostly agree.
- **Editorial:** examples or cross-references that contradict the text.

**Severity:**
- **Critical:** following the draft exactly can leak a credential, or act for the wrong User, account or Company.
- **High:** a security gap that only a careful implementer avoids, or an ambiguity that already breaks most pairs in our grid.
- **Medium:** undefined behaviour that implementations already handle differently, or a failure on a rarer path.
- **Low:** clarity, editorial or example fixes.

By severity: 5 Critical, 13 High, 30 Medium, 15 Low.

## 13 October 2026

One issue, from an audit of our Cloudflare costs and the adversarial scenarios it led to (XR, XT).

| ID | Area | Kind | Severity | Problem | Suggested fix | What we do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <a id="n46"></a>N46 | Conversations (7) and Operations | Security | Medium | **Nothing bounds how long a Company may keep an agent checking.** A conversation may say `working` or `queued` (7.7), or report `has_more` (7.5), for as long as it likes; an operation may stay `in_progress` with any `Retry-After` (Ops 6). The draft never says how long those states may last, that a Company should send events while it works, or that `Retry-After` is the least wait rather than a schedule. The Guides ask agents to "check for changes no more often than the task needs" but not what to do when the Company says it is busy. An agent that takes the Company at its word reads every second for ever, at its own cost (a hosted agent pays for the time it stays awake) and at the Company's. Our own agent did, until 13 October. Interop scenarios XR and XT. | Say a Company SHOULD send an event at least every few minutes while a conversation is `working` or `queued`, and SHOULD bound how long an operation stays `in_progress`; say `Retry-After` on a `200` is the least wait; say an agent MAY treat a busy conversation with no events as idle, MAY slow its reads of an unchanged operation, and SHOULD cap pages read per check. | `Retry-After` is the least wait; a busy conversation with nothing new for 2 minutes is read at the idle pace (30 s growing to 15 min, an hour after a day); an unchanged in-progress operation is read less often the longer it stays so; a read is at most 10 pages ([our reading](readings.md#pace)). |

## 12 October 2026

Two issues from an audit of our docs, just after midnight. Later that day we checked every row against the 12 October snapshot of the site: rows the text partly addresses got a dated note, N40 was corrected, the Severity column was added, and six issues that were our misreadings were withdrawn (S6, S15, S21, N36, N37 and N38).

| ID | Area | Kind | Severity | Problem | Suggested fix | What we do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <a id="n44"></a>N44 | Discovery (3) | Security | Medium | **When to fetch `poppy.json` and API descriptions again.** The draft says to cache discovery documents by their HTTP caching headers (a SHOULD, 3 and 3.2) but never says when an agent must fetch and check them again: before every Session, before a sign-in, or only when the cache expires. *Partly addressed, noted 12 Oct 2026:* the Guides bound the cache, "at least a minute and at most a day", so a removed key stops being trusted; nothing makes the bound normative or says every fetch is validated again. A Company, or whoever takes over its hosting, can move an endpoint between discovery and a later call (a "rug pull"), and an agent that keeps using a document it validated earlier never sees the move, while one that re-fetches mid-task may see a document it has never checked. The same goes for an OpenAPI description's `servers` and an MCP server's protected resource metadata. | Say an agent MUST NOT use a document past its cache lifetime without fetching and validating it again, that every fetch is validated as discovery is (domain, issuer and token hosts), and make the Guides' maximum lifetime normative. | We cache `poppy.json`, the issuer's metadata and each OpenAPI description by their caching headers, bounded to between a minute and a day, and validate each fetch in full; within that lifetime a moved endpoint isn't seen. Our adversarial `XK` attack was dropped for this reason (see [adversarial-interop.md](adversarial-interop.md#malicious-company-scenarios)). |
| <a id="n45"></a>N45 | Sign-in (4.4–4.9) | Security | High | **A lost code-exchange answer orphans an Account Token.** If the answer to the code exchange (4.5), or the device-code poll that succeeds (4.6), is lost (a timeout, a dropped connection, a 5xx from a proxy after the Company acted), the Company has signed the Session in and issued an Account Token the agent never received. The code is single-use, so it can't be exchanged again; the agent can't renew that Session, because renewing a signed-in Session needs the Account Token (and S5 leaves an assertion's answer undefined); and it can't revoke the token, because revocation needs the token. The grant stays live until it expires, and the User sees an account connected to an agent that doesn't hold it. The draft has no idempotent exchange and no recovery. See also N5. | Let a retried exchange with the same code, verifier and `session_id` return the same answer for a short window, or let an agent revoke by `session_id` with its client assertion; say a Company ends the Session's grant when that happens. | We abandon that Session (it is never renewed with the assertion) and start a new one; the card says the sign-in was interrupted, and the User signs in again. The orphaned grant is left to expire at the Company. |

| <a id="n43"></a>N43 | Browsing (5) | Security | High | **Website actions outside Operations have no approval step.** Once the agent's browser joins the Session (5) it can submit any form on the Company's website (place an order, change an address, cancel a plan), and nothing in section 5 says which submissions are consequential or that the User must approve them. An agent that gates API writes can be routed around through the website, and a Company can't tell an approved submission from an unapproved one. *Partly addressed, noted 12 Oct 2026:* a Company can already route a website action through Operations (Ops 1, "an API call, an MCP tool, a conversation, or a web page"), and Ops 4.4 says that when the website confirms an operation "the Personal Agent's duties in 5.1 apply however it confirms". | Say that a Personal Agent MUST get the User's approval before a website submission that a write through the Company's API would need approved, or let a Company mark forms (or URLs) as consequential. | Every form submission waits for the User's approval on a card listing exactly what will be sent, and exactly that is sent; `click` follows links only, so it can't submit a form. |

### Evening: the interop grid

Found by the interop grid. N36, N37 and N38 came from the same run; they were withdrawn on 12 October.

| ID | Area | Kind | Severity | Problem | Suggested fix | What we do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <a id="n39"></a>N39 | Sessions (4.2, 4.3, 4.8) | Interop | Medium | **The error for an ended Session at a resource.** *Partly addressed, noted 12 Oct 2026:* 4.2 does say where for renewal, "If it has ended, the Company returns `invalid_session`, and the Personal Agent starts a new Session", and lists `invalid_session` in the token endpoint's errors. What a resource returns for an ended Session is undefined: section 6 lists only `invalid_token`, `sign_in_required` and `insufficient_scope` (S13). Grid: Datalayer answers an ended Session with `invalid_token` everywhere, including the token endpoint, against 4.2; pi-poppy's agent renews on `invalid_token`, gets `invalid_session` from the token endpoint, and gives up instead of starting a new Session, also against 4.2. | `invalid_session` at resources too. | Start a new Session on `invalid_session` from either; renew on `invalid_token` at a resource. |
| <a id="n40"></a>N40 | Conversations (7) | Interop | Medium | **`wait` is a hint, but examples read like a promise.** The home page walkthrough's sends with `wait` all return the Company's reply in the response. *Corrected and partly addressed, 12 Oct 2026:* we had said 7.3's example returns the reply, but it shows only the request. 7.3 says the send "responds as a read of the conversation's events from that cursor would (7.5)", and 7.5 says the Company "MAY return sooner, or cap `wait`", so an empty `events` list is a valid answer. Not said: that the agent then reads events for the reply. Grid: poppyseed honours `wait` only on `GET …/events`, so an agent that expects the reply from the send never sees it. | Say the agent MUST read events for a reply the send didn't return, and show an example with no reply. | Read in the background when the reply isn't in the response. |
| <a id="n41"></a>N41 | Operations | Interop | Medium | **`null` for an optional member.** Nothing says whether an optional member may be `null` rather than absent, here or in the core protocol. Grid: Datalayer sends `"expires_at": null` on confirm and `"result": {"data": null}` on cancel; pi-poppy rejects both, so it reports a performed operation as failed. | Omit optional members; receivers treat `null` as absent. | Treat `null` as absent. |
| <a id="n42"></a>N42 | Privacy and security | Security | Medium | **No test mode.** The SSRF rules (Guides) say "Reject loopback, private, and link-local addresses unless you've configured them on purpose", so an implementation can be tested against another only through a configured relaxation, and each invents its own: poppyseed's `allowInsecureClients`, AuthKit's `allowInsecureClientIds` (which also allows `http` `client_id`s), pi-poppy's and Datalayer's private-address settings. Broad switches like these are easy to leave on in production. *Partly addressed, noted 12 Oct 2026:* we had said the Guides forbid them outright; the exception is there, but nothing bounds it. | A Guides note: a test mode limited to a named reserved suffix (such as `.test`), HTTPS only, off by default. | Our harness names `*.interop.test` and needed no relaxation for our client; see [interop-spec.md](interop-spec.md) §3. |

## Open topics, with our view

The draft lists these three on [Path to 1.0](https://personalagentprotocol.org/docs/path-to-1#open-topics); until 12 October they had a page of their own.

- **Payments.** Ops already covers approval. A payment extension could sit on Ops, with amounts in a standard `{amount: string, currency}`, which would also fix S20 and N33.
- **Push notifications.** We poll: every second for 2 minutes after activity in a busy conversation, otherwise every 30 seconds to 15 minutes, or hourly after a quiet day ([N46](#n46)). Suggest a `notification_uri` in client metadata, receiving signed, content-free "events available" pings, after which the agent reads with its cursor.
- **Attachments.** HTTPS URLs on the Company's hosts, fetched with DPoP, with a declared type and size. Forms go through Ops or `url`.
- **Missing from the list:**
  - version negotiation (N22);
  - a security considerations section (N15);
  - resetting a User ID (N12);
  - a shared conformance suite (see [interop-testing.md](interop-testing.md));
  - delegation, such as family accounts.
